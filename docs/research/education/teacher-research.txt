**OpenAI’s Intelligent UI** (launched with GPT-6 around early October 2026) lets ChatGPT generate responses that mix text with interactive visuals—such as tappable diagrams, charts, forms, buttons, calculators, side-by-side comparisons, timelines, maps, and small tools—directly in the conversation. It automatically chooses the best format for the query (or sticks to plain text when that’s more useful).[](<https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001598-intelligent-ui-in-chatgpt>)

It is available in the standard **Chat** tab of ChatGPT (free and paid plans, via GPT-6 models). It does **not** work in the Work tab, Voice mode, or with the Pro reasoning option (GPT-6 Astra).[](<https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001598-intelligent-ui-in-chatgpt>)

### How teachers can use Intelligent UI effectively
Prompt naturally—Intelligent UI activates when visuals or interactivity add value. Be specific about grade level, topic, learning goals, and desired interactivity for better results. Upload curriculum files, standards, or notes for context.

- “Explain the water cycle / plant cell / fractions / photosynthesis for 4th graders with an interactive diagram I can tap for details.”

Here are **10 common pain points** specifically relevant to Grade 12 (senior/high school final-year) teachers, drawn from recent surveys, studies, and teacher reports across multiple countries:

1. **Extreme workload and poor work-life balance**  
   Heavy grading (especially essays, projects, and final exams), lesson planning for advanced content, administrative tasks, and often extra after-school or weekend revision classes leave little personal time.

2. **Student motivation and “senioritis”**  
   Declining engagement, work refusal, and a sense among many students that the year is “already over” or that grades no longer matter as much once college applications are submitted.

3. **High-stakes exam and graduation pressure**  
   Intense focus on covering the full curriculum for exit exams (e.g., matric, A-levels, state tests, AP), often forcing rushed teaching and teaching-to-the-test rather than deeper learning.

4. **Supporting student mental health and anxiety**  
   Grade 12 students frequently experience high stress related to college admissions, future uncertainty, exam pressure, and identity transitions. Teachers often become informal counselors with limited training or support.

5. **Wide range of student readiness and ability levels**  
   Classes mix highly motivated college-bound students with those who have significant learning gaps, low engagement, or different post-secondary paths, making differentiation extremely difficult.

6. **Classroom management with older adolescents**  
   Dealing with greater student independence, occasional defiance or disrespect, digital distractions (phones), and more complex social/emotional dynamics than in younger grades.

7. **Limited resources and large class sizes**  
   Insufficient up-to-date materials, technology, or specialist support for advanced or specialized subjects, compounded by overcrowded classrooms.

8. **Administrative and compliance burden**  
   Tracking graduation requirements, intervention documentation, parent communications, data reporting, and accountability measures that pull time away from teaching.

9. **Out-of-field teaching and content mastery demands**  
   Being assigned subjects outside one’s specialization (common in some systems for senior electives), while also needing deep expertise for rigorous Grade 12 content.

10. **Emotional labor and burnout risk**  
    Constant pressure from results expectations, parent demands around college outcomes, supporting students through major life transitions, and feeling under-supported or under-compensated relative to the intensity of the role.

This is a detailed reverse-engineering of how OpenAI’s Intelligent UI actually works under the hood:

- The model writes interfaces in a custom language called **DIL** (Markdown + JSX-like tags + JavaScript for state/logic)

- A sandboxed client runtime (Web Worker + iframe) executes it
- ChatGPT’s native components render the result
- Local interactions (sliders, buttons, etc.) update without calling the model again
- Streaming, design system/catalog (~70 components), state management, and safety measures are all covered
